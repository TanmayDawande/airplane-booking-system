Assessment 8: front end and database connectivity

About 3 minutes
Teammate 1Introduction and interface walkthrough. On camera.
  1. Introduction (0:00 to 0:45). State the team and presenter names: "Hello, our DBMS project is the Airline Reservation System designed in 3NF to support flight search, seat reservations, and transactional payments."
  2. Front-end interface (0:45 to 1:30). Walk through the home page, the flight search form (origin, destination, date, cabin), the departures list and the navigation bar.
  3. Database connectivity (1:30 to 2:00). Click the database engine badge in the top header. It shows the backend is connected to MySQL or SQLite, with the host and the number of active tables.
Teammate 2Data entry and table verification. On camera.
  1. Enter a record through the front end (2:00 to 2:40). Open Admin and click Add passenger. Fill in a new record:
    Name: Rohit Verma | Email: rohit.verma@example.com | Phone: +91-9876543210 | Passport: Z1234567
    Then click Add passenger in the dialog.
  2. Retrieve the new record (2:40 to 3:00). Point to the green confirmation box. It shows the generated primary key (passenger_id) and timestamp read back from the database.
  3. Verify in the table (3:00 to 3:45). Open the SQL studio and click the passengers table, or run:
    SELECT * FROM passengers WHERE email = 'rohit.verma@example.com';
    Show the row to confirm it is stored.

Assessment 9: database operations and back end

About 4 minutes
Teammate 1Back-end communication and full CRUD.
  1. Back-end architecture (0:00 to 0:45). Explain that Flask talks to the database through parameterized queries, which guard against SQL injection and keep transactions clean.
  2. INSERT. On the Admin page, click Schedule flight. Enter flight AI-999 from BOM to DEL with a base fare of ₹5,200. Submit it and point out the new flight in the table.
  3. UPDATE. Click Edit on AI-999. Change the fare to ₹5,600 and the status to Delayed, then save. Show the new values in the table, and verify them in the SQL studio:
    SELECT flight_number, base_fare, status FROM flights WHERE flight_number = 'AI-999';
  4. DELETE. Click Delete on AI-999 and confirm the dialog. The row disappears from the table, and SELECT * FROM flights WHERE flight_number = 'AI-999' returns 0 rows.
Teammate 2Complex SQL queries and triggers.
  1. Multi-table INNER JOIN. Open Queries and reports. Show the five-table JOIN across bookings, passengers, flights, airlines and airports, and explain how foreign keys keep the tables linked without duplicating data.
  2. GROUP BY and aggregates with HAVING. Show the airline revenue table. Explain how SQL totals bookings (COUNT), gross revenue (SUM) and average fare (AVG), with HAVING gross_revenue > 5000.
  3. Nested subquery and triggers. Show the high-value passengers report, built on WHERE total_amount > (SELECT AVG(total_amount) FROM bookings). Finish on the Audit log tab in Admin, where database triggers record each insert and update.

Assessment 10: complete workflow and validation

About 5 minutes
Teammate 1Search, seat selection and passenger entry.
  1. Search flights (0:00 to 0:45). On the home page, choose Mumbai (BOM) to New Delhi (DEL) and click Search flights.
  2. Select a flight and seat (0:45 to 2:00). Click Select seat on AI-101. On the cabin map, point out the occupied seats (hatched) and the open ones, then click seat 11C. Show the price breakdown update for that seat.
  3. Input validation (2:00 to 2:30). Submit with incomplete or badly formatted fields to show client-side and server-side validation. Then enter valid passenger details.
  4. Submit the transaction. Click Confirm & Pay Now, and point out that this runs as one atomic ACID transaction.
Teammate 2Boarding pass, database verification and cancellation.
  1. Boarding pass (2:30 to 3:15). Show the generated pass with its PNR, passenger name, seat 11C, departure time and barcode. Click Print or save as PDF.
  2. Verify the transaction (3:15 to 4:00). Note the PNR. Open the SQL studio and run:
    SELECT b.pnr, b.seat_number, b.status, p.amount, p.payment_status
    FROM bookings b JOIN payments p ON b.booking_id = p.booking_id
    WHERE b.pnr = '<YOUR_PNR>';
    Both the booking and the payment rows exist, so the two inserts committed together.
  3. Cancel and release the seat (4:00 to 4:45). Open My bookings and click Cancel booking. The status changes to CANCELLED, the payment is refunded and seat 11C is free to book again.
  4. Conclusion (4:45 to 5:00). All three assessments have a separate, verified demonstration that covers the DBMS curriculum.
